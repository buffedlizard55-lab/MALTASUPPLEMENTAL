Border paperwork: the one thing to recheck before you fly

  • EES (Entry/Exit System) — two positions, unresolved. The EU states it has been fully operational at external borders since 10 April 2026, recording a facial image and four fingerprints for most non-EU short-stay visitors. Reporting in September 2026 says nine Schengen states, including Malta, asked the Commission for more time and were informally allowed to keep pausing biometric capture with no deadline set, and Malta’s own airport page describes arrivals being randomly selected for a biometric lane. Full conflict →
  • ETIAS — not in operation, but your trip sits on the boundary. The official EU site says ETIAS is “currently not in operation”, collects no applications and gives no start date. Industry and government sources have long said “last quarter of 2026” — which is exactly when you travel — while a September 2026 report says the EU has stopped printing that date and points to 2027. Practical advice: recheck the EU page in the week before departure. If a launch date lands before 13 October, the honest read is that a transitional period follows any launch, so keep your passport, return ticket and accommodation proof ready either way. Never pay a third-party site claiming to issue an ETIAS. Full status →
  • Allow time at both ends. Malta’s Home Affairs Minister was reported saying EES resources were “already at full capacity” and that registration runs about 40 seconds to a minute and a half per passenger, after 40-minute-plus immigration delays were reported in Malta. That is April 2026 reporting, not a forecast for your flight — treat it as a reason to leave yourself slack, not a promise.

The U.S. government's own read on Malta, and where to find help

The State Department's advisory for Malta is Level 1 — Exercise normal precautions, issued 9 July 2026, and says Malta is generally a safe destination for travelers. Two of its own cautions are worth acting on: rip currents have caused drownings among tourists, so follow lifeguards and local beach warnings, and a separate worldwide caution asks Americans everywhere to exercise increased caution.

Consular contact is unusually local: the U.S. Embassy is at Ta'Qali National Park Street, Attard ATD 4000 — the same Ta' Qali/Attard area as the CS2 venue. Main and after-hours telephone +356-2561-4000, email ACSMalta@state.gov. Enrol in STEP before you fly so the embassy can reach you or your emergency contact, and buy travel insurance covering medical care, evacuation and cancellation.

Source: U.S. State Department — Malta travel advisory, read 11 October 2026.
