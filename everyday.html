Everyday life in Malta — the US-citizen briefing

You live in California; Malta is an EU island that was a British colony until 1964. That mix explains almost everything on this page: UK-style plugs, left-hand traffic, English everywhere, the euro, and a 9-hour time jump from Pacific. Every item below is checked against the linked source; estimates and community-sourced tips are labelled.

Trip window 13–20 Oct 2026 (CEST, UTC+2) From SFO (PDT, UTC−7) — +9 hours Emergency 112

1Plugs & power — Type G, 230 V, 50 Hz

Pack a Type G adapter (UK-style), not a European one Malta uses the British three-rectangular-pin plug (Type G) at 230 V / 50 Hz — the same sockets as the UK, a legacy of British rule. Continental-Europe two-round-pin plugs don't fit either; as a US traveller you need a Type G (or universal) travel adapter. Sockets often have their own on/off switch — flick it if a device isn't charging.

3Language, money, VAT & tipping

Language

English is official

Maltese and English are both official languages; laws are enacted in both; English is taught in schools and used everywhere a visitor needs it — signs, menus, hospitals. No phrasebook needed (a few Maltese words are appreciated: Merħba = welcome).

Money

Euro (€)

Malta adopted the euro in 2008. Cards are widely accepted, but per the State Department: American Express is not widely accepted — carry Visa/Mastercard. ATMs accept US bank cards but frequently charge an ATM fee.

Taxes

VAT 18% standard

Standard VAT is 18% (reduced 7% on hotel accommodation, 5% on some food/essentials). As elsewhere in the EU, consumer prices on shelves and menus display VAT-inclusive amounts — the number you see is the number you pay.

Tipping

Optional — not 15–20%

Tipping is not obligatory and there is no US-style expectation. Common practice: round up the bill or leave ~5–10% at table service if no service charge is on the bill; small change at cafés/bars; rounding up taxis. Check the bill for a service charge first.

5The British-legacy quirks (vs. everyday US life)

  • Drive on the left. If you rent a car, the wheel is on the right side of the car and you keep left — plus narrow, congested roads (State Department embassy tips). Most visitors skip the car entirely; see Getting Around.
  • British plugs and switched sockets — §1 above.
  • Distances are tiny. Malta is ~27 km long; “cross-town” errands are 20-minute bus rides, not US-style drives.
  • Clock: Malta is on Central European Summer Time (CEST, UTC+2) for your whole trip — daylight saving in the EU ends Sunday 25 October 2026, after you leave. That's 9 hours ahead of San Francisco (PDT): 09:00 in SFO = 18:00 in Malta. Call home in the morning California time.
  • Phones: country code +356. Check your US plan's international rates; a Maltese SIM/eSIM for the week is usually cheaper (budget ~$10–15 — Estimate, see Costs).
  • Power frequency is 50 Hz (US is 60 Hz) — relevant only for motor-driven US appliances; solid-state chargers don't care.
  • Flag — not verified Shop/opening-hour patterns (Sunday trading, afternoon closures) were deliberately not stated here: no official source was checked for 2026 hours. Confirm on each venue's own page or Google listing before relying on them.

6Packing checklist (from this page)

  • Type G adapter (one per device-user; a multi-USB adapter beats fighting over one socket)
  • Visa/Mastercard (not Amex) + some euro cash for markets, taxis and small cafés
  • Universal power strip — one Type G plug in, several US devices out
  • Light evening layer + sunscreen + swimwear (October sea ~23 °C)
  • Prescription meds in original packaging with the prescription (State Department guidance)
  • Travel insurance policy printout (prize excludes it — Terms §5)
  • Saved numbers: 112, embassy +356 2561 4000
  • Photocopy/phone photo of passport separate from the passport itself
