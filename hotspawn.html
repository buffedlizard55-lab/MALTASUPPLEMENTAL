1Who is Hotspawn?

  • An esports media platform (hotspawn.com) — “your home for everything esports”: news, guides, interviews, tournament coverage across LoL, VALORANT, CS2, Dota 2. Self-described as fan-built; WordPress-based site with reCAPTCHA, active daily publishing, and a newsletter signup.
  • Official TWC partner: the 16 Sep 2026 PR Newswire release (fetched directly) states verbatim: “Hotspawn returns as a Presenting Partner of the Thunderpick World Championship 2026, supporting coverage and content around the tournament and its competing teams.” Hotspawn's own 19 May 2025 post announced the original TWC 2025 presenting partnership, and a 17 Dec 2025 post says it's official media partner for 2026 and 2027. Hotspawn also publishes the TWC series hub on its own site.
  • It runs the Hotspawn Community (community.hotspawn.com) — where the giveaway lives — and the Pick'Ems game platform (play.hotspawn.com) with its own weekly Steam-voucher prizes. The giveaway T&Cs say they govern the “Hotspawn Community Thunderpick World Championship Giveaway.”
  • Legal identity: unknown. The Terms of Use (last modified 21.03.2025) refer only to “the Provider” — no company name anywhere — and set governing law/jurisdiction as England and Wales. The Privacy Policy is the unmodified WordPress template (no data controller, no registered address, no DPO). A UK Companies House search for “hotspawn” returns zero results (verified directly 23 Sep 2026) — no company on the UK register carries that registered name. (A verified negative: it doesn't prove absence, since a company can trade under a different registered name, but it does exhaust the register route to identifying the Promoter.)
  • Domain record (RDAP, checked 23 Sep 2026): hotspawn.com was registered 5 Jun 2017 via UK registrar Safenames Ltd, expires 5 Jun 2027, DNS on Cloudflare; registrant identity is redacted. A nine-year-old domain with a UK registrar is consistent with an established UK-linked publisher, but it still does not name the legal entity behind the “Provider”/“Promoter”.
  • Giveaway T&Cs page currently unreachable: community.hotspawn.com (including the /terms URL for the giveaway) returned “no available server” on 23 Sep 2026, and the Wayback Machine holds only a same-day placeholder capture — no archived copy of the terms exists. The clause-by-clause analysis below was made from the page while it was live; ask Sophie for a PDF copy of the T&Cs so you hold a fixed version. Flag — high
  • Contradictory data terms: the same Terms of Use claim compliance with “international standards, Canadian privacy laws, and EU regulations (GDPR)” and state “The Controller does not transfer any Personal Data outside the European Economic Area” — while the giveaway is being run for a platform whose parent betting brand is Curaçao-licensed and whose winners could be US residents. Your data would necessarily cross the Atlantic. None of this is evidence of fraud; it's evidence of unreviewed legal paperwork.

  • 100 entries total; draw on 1 September; 3 winners, random. The winner list is public on the official giveaway page: #1 = you (extradrummy1913), followed by two other publicly listed handles (not reproduced here — they're third parties' data; open the source link if you need them).
  • Three winners from 100 entries is plausible, not suspicious; the random draw means XP farming changed your odds but the win was a draw, not a ranking. Your Pick'Ems leaderboard position (8th with 89 points at snapshot time) is a separate, unrelated contest.

7Verdict & the safe-disclosure plan

Is it safe to provide your information?

Staged, yes. Wholesale, not yet. The people, the event, the win and the prize are all corroborated by primary sources, and no scam indicator appears. But the binding document says you're ineligible, the Promoter is unnamed, and the privacy terms are boilerplate. So:

  • Now (safe): your full name, date of birth, phone number, preferred departure airport (SFO) — the standard travel-booking set. These match your passport and booking needs.
  • Now (your choice): mailing address = the owned-property address (see Entry & Address). Never a false one; never presented as your “residence” for any eligibility purpose.
  • Hold back until the waiver is in writing: passport scan, proof-of-residence documents, and any “confirm you meet the eligibility requirements” checkbox that you'd have to answer honestly as a US resident. If they insist on proof of residence before confirming the prize, that's the moment the clause 1.1 fight becomes concrete — get the written waiver first.
  • Demand in writing (email is fine): (1) waiver of the clause 1.1 residency requirement for you specifically, signed by a named person at a named company; (2) the Promoter's legal entity + registered address; (3) confirmation that flights and hotel are booked and paid directly by them, not reimbursed to you; (4) a secure channel for any document they do need; (5) your ticket's day-coverage and the hotel address.
  • Never pay anything to claim, process, or “secure” the prize — the T&Cs require no payment, so any invoice is a scam signal per FTC guidance (report at reportfraud.ftc.gov).
  • Verify every sender is exactly @hotspawn.com; treat the off-brand .io domain and any lookalike addresses as hostile until proven otherwise.
  • Don't spend a dollar of your own on travel (no deposits, no non-refundable bookings) until items 1–3 above are in writing. Your worst case otherwise is ~$2,500–4,700 (see Costs).
