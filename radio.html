  3. On block 6A, find BBC World Service and confirm Sportsworld on 17–18 October (about 15:06 and 16:06 Malta time).
  4. On FM, try NET FM 101 MHz for its weekend sports grid and Vibe FM 88.7 MHz for Malta Premier League coverage.

Receiver quick check — what actually works coming from the US

  • Analog FM works. Maltese national FM stations sit inside the international FM broadcast band, which the ITU GE84 plan sets at 87.5–108 MHz for Region 1. Check the tuning range printed on any radio before relying on it; most phone FM chips are disabled in software.
  • Do not bring a US HD Radio set. The United States adopted HD Radio, a different digital standard; HD Radio and DAB/DAB+ receivers cannot decode each other’s signals.
  • DAB+ (the priority) needs a DAB+ receiver bought in a DAB+ market. Scan all three national blocks — 6A (181.936 MHz), 6C (185.360 MHz) and 12A (223.936 MHz) — look for “Sports Channel” and BBC World Service, and buy the radio locally: the BBC states that a DAB+ radio purchased in Malta is necessary because UK DAB radios use a different codec. Directory entries are not an operator schedule and no signal was tested at the hotel, so confirm what actually plays on the receiver.
  • FM to try first: NET FM at 101 MHz for its three weekend sports slots (Friday 18:30, Saturday 16:00, Sunday 15:00), the last two landing on the Premier League kick-off windows, and Vibe FM at 88.7 MHz, the published Malta Premier League radio partner.
  • AM / medium wave: no current Malta sports broadcast was verified. Do not rely on historic references to Radju Malta on 999 kHz.
  • Streams are not radio. Thunderpick’s Twitch, Kick and YouTube channels and any station app depend on mobile data, roaming cost and territory rules, and they are not an over-the-air option.
