When your itinerary arrives — the 10-minute intake

Everything on this page is blank on purpose. When the confirmed itinerary lands, work down this list once and the planner is done.

  1. Flights & dates (rows: flights, hotel-stay): enter the booking totals, the exact check-in/check-out dates and the number of billable nights. Update every daily row to match the real dates.
  2. Hotel extras (hotel-separate-charges): read the invoice. The official basis is €1.50 per night for each guest aged 18+ (cap €22.50 per person per visit) — six nights is about €9 per eligible adult, but enter the figure the hotel actually bills, and put anything else separately charged (deposits, resort fees, city tax lines) here.
  3. Event access (event-access): leave blank unless the organizer confirms a ticket. No public sale for the Thunderpick Finals was found on 11 October 2026, and the matches stream on Thunderpick’s Twitch, Kick and YouTube channels — so a zero here is legitimate.
  4. Airport transfers (airport-transfer-arrival / -departure) and bus rows (local-bus-and-cards, event-commute): budget from the published prices — €2.50 per single day-route journey, €3.00 night/Tallinja Direct, €3.50 on TD1, €19 for 12 journeys, €25 for the 7-day Explore Adult card — then overwrite with real receipts. A 7-day Explore card for the whole stay is often cheaper than repeated singles.
  5. Taxi fallback (taxi-rideshare): no quote exists until you open Bolt or eCabs in Malta. Enter the live quote for one Qawra–Ta’ Qali trip and multiply by the number of late nights you actually plan.
  6. Food, activities and gaming (food-and-drink, activities-and-sightseeing, gaming-and-arcade): set one daily allowance and put the whole-party amount in the aggregate row, or itemise in the seven daily rows — never both for the same spending.
  7. Connectivity and money (mobile-connectivity, bank-cash-fees): enter the eSIM/SIM price you actually buy and your bank’s foreign-transaction fee as a percentage-based estimate on planned card spend.
  8. Insurance, passports and contingency (travel-insurance, passport-entry-costs, other-incidentals): enter real premium/purchase amounts, then leave a deliberate contingency figure.
  9. Finish: check the Difference card (actual minus budget), then press Download CSV backup. The planner is then a trip record as well as a plan.

Price references come from the transport guide; they are the operator’s published prices, not quotes for your specific journeys.

Quick-use notes

  • Each line has a budget and actual amount, both in euros. Use whole-trip/whole-party totals consistently; if you prefer per-person amounts, note that basis and apply it to every row.
  • The reference links are for reconfirming details or choosing a provider; they are not price quotes. No amount is prefilled, even where another guide page reports a dated menu or ticket price.
  • The event-access row is only a placeholder. A tournament schedule does not establish public access or a ticket price; leave it blank until the organizer confirms.
  • Use the transport guide, food shortlist, activities and events, and everyday guide to compare sourced options before entering your own amounts.
