3Maltese classics — the food to actually try

National snack

Pastizzi

Diamond flaky pastry, ricotta or peas, from ~€0.80–1.50, available from 6 am at every pastizzerija. Warm is best. Is-Serkin (Crystal Palace Bar, Valletta) is the classic name.

UNESCO-listed

Ftira

Ring-shaped Maltese bread topped with tuna, sardines, tomato & capers — ftira was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2020. Try Nenu the Artisan Baker (Valletta); Qormi's ~50 bakeries are a pilgrimage.

National dish

Stuffat tal-Fenek

Rabbit stewed in red wine, garlic, tomato, olives — the emblem of Maltese cuisine, best in village restaurants. (See Ta Bertu's above for the top-reviewed modern take.)

Street

Bragioli, ġbejniet, imqaret

Braised beef rolls with spinach & pork; ricotta-flour dumplings (vegetarian); date & cheese fritters. Plus Kinnie (quinine drink), fish pie, and bigilla.

Verified 23 Sep 2026 maltadventures.com Maltese-cuisine guide, jesseatstheworld.com, AFAR's "best food in Malta" — all consistent on dishes and the named spots. Flag Exact pastizzi prices vary by bakery; “from ~€1” is the guide-consensus, not a fixed price.

5Eating on the $30/day budget

$30/day in Malta is workable but tight A sit-down dinner in Sliema or St Julian's alone can run €20–30 before drinks. To make $30/day (≈€26) work:
  • Breakfast: pastizzi + coffee (~€3–5)
  • Lunch: ftira/tuna or pastizzerija-style meal, or a café plate (~€6–10)
  • Dinner: share or take from a mid-range trattoria's lunch menu when possible; the €15–20 mains are where the budget lives or dies
The Top-10 guide restaurants are a one-night splurge, not a weekly rhythm. Full model on the Costs & Budget page.
