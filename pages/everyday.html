Electricity: Plugs & Voltage

Important: Malta uses the UK-style Type G plug and operates on 230V (50Hz).

As a US traveler, you will need a physical plug adapter. Your laptop and phone charger are likely dual-voltage (check for "100-240V" printed on them) and will only need the adapter. Hair dryers or clippers from the US without dual voltage will fry if used without a proper step-down voltage converter.

Money & Tipping

Currency: Euro (€). Cash is rarely needed for big purchases, as Visa/Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere. American Express is less commonly accepted.

Tipping: In the US, 20% is standard; in Malta, tipping is more modest. Leaving 5% to 10% for good service at restaurants is appreciated, provided a service charge hasn't already been added.

Connectivity (SIM/eSIM)

To avoid exorbitant US carrier roaming fees, make sure your phone is unlocked and download an eSIM (such as Airalo or Holafly) before your trip. Alternatively, you can buy a physical prepaid SIM from local providers like Epic, GO, or Melita upon arrival at the airport.

Language & Communication

Maltese and English are the co-official languages. English is spoken fluently by virtually everyone, so you will have no language barrier navigating, reading menus, or interacting with locals.

Health & Safety

Emergency Number: 112

Malta is generally a very safe destination. Violent crime is extremely low. Tap water is perfectly safe to drink, but because it is largely desalinated seawater, it can have a distinct taste. Many visitors and locals prefer buying bottled water for drinking.

Weather in October

The weather during your trip (mid-October) is typically beautiful and mild. Highs hover around 75°F-77°F (24°C-25°C) and lows around 63°F-64°F (17°C-18°C). Pack mostly summer clothing but bring a light jacket or sweater for the evenings.
