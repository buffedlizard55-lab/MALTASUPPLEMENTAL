Landmarks & sights worth your 8 days

Malta is small — you can hit the big sights in three days and still have room for Gozo or Comino. Your base (Sliema/St Julian's per the likely prize itinerary) is a ferry-ride from Valletta, and the venue in Ta' Qali sits in the middle of the island, so nothing is more than ~30 minutes away.

1Valletta — the capital (UNESCO World Heritage)

  • St John's Co-Cathedral — the Knights' Baroque cathedral on Republic Street; Caravaggio's beheading of St John is inside. The single most-photographed interior in the country.
  • Upper Barrakka Gardens — free, the classic panorama over the Grand Harbour (and the spot to shoot the Three Cities).
  • Fort St Elmo — now the National War Museum and National Archives; small, steeply raked museum, worth 1.5–2 hours.
  • Republic Street, Merchant Street, the Lower Barrakka terraces — the UNESCO core; add Caffè Cordina (1837) and a pastizzi stop at Is-Serkin.
  • You can walk all of Valletta in a morning; the ferry from Sliema (or Vaporetto) drops you at the City Gate.

2Mdina & Rabat — the Silent City

  • Mdina — the walled medieval “Silent City” on a central hilltop; lane-walking, cathedral (with a Caravaggio), bastion viewpoints over the island. Go mid-morning before tour buses; no cars except one bus a day.
  • Rabat (at the gate) — the Rabat St Paul's Catacombs (Roman-era, one of the largest underground necropoles in the western Mediterranean) and St Paul's Grotto.
  • Parking is scarce — take a bus (see Getting Around).

3The Three Cities (Vittoriosa, Senglea, Cospicua)

  • Across the harbour from Sliema: baroque facades, the Malta Maritime Museum, Fort St Angelo in Vittoriosa (Birgu), and the iconic “Bastion Point” photo from Cospicua.
  • BirguFest by Candlelight is held here in October — but Flag the 2026 dates are unconfirmed: four sources disagree (9–10, 9–11, 11–13, and 16–17 Oct). Confirm with the Birgu Local Council before building a plan around it; if it lands on 16–17 Oct it overlaps event day 3.

5Sea, cliffs & nearby isles

  • Blue Grotto (Zurrieq) — the glowing sea cave; boat rides from the Zurrieq pier, weather-dependent, morning light is best.
  • Dingli Cliffs — the highest sea cliffs on Malta (~250 m); a short walk to the clifftop village of Dingli.
  • Mosta Dome — one of Europe's largest domes; the church centre of Malta.
  • Marsaxlokk — the fishing village with luzzu-painted boats and a Saturday street market.
  • Popeye Village (Anchor Bay) — the 1980 film set, now a theme park; quick stop if you want something unusual.
  • Comino — the small island with the Cristal Lagoon (swimming/snorkelling); ferries from Cirkewwa (see Getting Around for fares). Sea temperature in October is still ~23 °C — wetsuit optional.
  • Gozo — a half-day or full day: the Cittadella in Victoria, Ramla Bay, Dwejra's “Inland Sea”. Ferries: Valletta fast ferry (€7.50 one way) or Cirkewwa (€4.65 return walk-on).
  • Ta' Qali Crafts Village — right next to the tournament venue; a good off-matchday filler (craft workshops, food).
