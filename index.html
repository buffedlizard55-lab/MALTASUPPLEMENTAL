  • Everyday essentials: Malta uses Type G sockets at 230 V / 50 Hz, so US plugs need an adapter, and single-voltage high-wattage appliances also need a converter. Government of Malta and U.S. State Department sources confirm dialing 112 for emergencies. Hotels collect a €1.50-per-night eco-contribution for guests 18+ at the start of the visit (cap €22.50 per person per visit, charged per night the room is made available and shown separately on the receipt; the MTA guidelines' fifteen-day figure defines an “interruption” in non-licensable premises, not a minimum stay), so six nights implies about €9 per eligible adult — conditional on the invoice. Customs allowances: the EU travellers’ page for entry (€430 goods allowance by air/sea plus alcohol and tobacco limits) and U.S. CBP for the trip home ($800 returning-resident exemption after 48 hours, 200 cigarettes / 100 cigars). EES: the official EU position (fully operational at external borders since 10 April 2026) conflicts with September 2026 reporting that nine Schengen states — including Malta — asked for and were informally granted more time to run biometric capture, and Malta’s airport page still describes arrivals being randomly selected for a biometric lane. That conflict is recorded, not resolved, so allow generous border time either way. ETIAS: the official EU site says it is not in operation, takes no applications and gives no start date — but industry and government sources have long targeted “last quarter of 2026”, which is exactly your travel window, while a September 2026 report says that target has been dropped. Recheck the EU page the week you fly, and never pay a third-party site claiming to issue one.
