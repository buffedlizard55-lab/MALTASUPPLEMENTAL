Entry & immigration — and the address question answered

Two jobs on this page: (1) exactly what a US citizen faces entering Malta in October 2026, rule by rule with official sources; (2) the answer to “what address can I give them?” — including your privacy concern about the address on your California driver's licence.

1Your entry checklist (US citizen, 7 nights, tourism)

RequirementRuleStatus / what to do
Visa US citizens need no visa for stays ≤ 90 days in the Schengen area Verified US State Department Malta advisory (Level 1, 9 Jul 2026). Nothing to do.
ETIAS EU's electronic travel authorisation — not in operation Verified The official EU ETIAS page says it is not in operation and no applications are collected; the “last quarter 2026” target was removed from the official page in mid-July 2026, with 2027 now the most likely launch. You do not need it for 13–20 Oct. Any website charging you for an “ETIAS” today is a scam. Re-check travel-europe.europa.eu/etias a week before departure in case a Q4 launch with a grace period is announced.
EES biometrics EU Entry/Exit System — fully operational at all external borders since 10 April 2026 Verified Official EU travel portal: on first entry you'll give 4 fingerprints + a facial image, replace the old stamp; 90 days in any 180 counted as one period across EES countries. Budget extra time at your first Schengen-entry airport — note there is no nonstop SFO→MLA, so if you connect through a Schengen hub (Frankfurt, Paris CDG, Copenhagen, Zurich, Rome…), EES registration happens there, not in Malta; Malta is your first Schengen entry only with a non-Schengen connection (e.g. Dublin). Refusing biometrics means refused entry.
Passport Valid at least 3 months beyond your date of departure from Schengen, and issued within the previous 10 years — the two conditions the Borders Code actually sets. (No blank-page minimum appears in the Code, and the State Dept's Malta summary lists only the 3-month rule; keeping a couple of empty pages is sensible, not a legal requirement.) Verified Schengen Borders Code Art. 6(1) (EUR-Lex). If yours expires before ~Jan 2027, renew: adult renewal $130 (+$60 expedited, refundable if the timeframe is missed); routine 4–6 weeks, expedited 2–3 weeks — so act within days, not weeks (State Department fees/processing pages).
Means of subsistence Malta's border practice: minimum €48 per day for the duration of the visit Verified European Commission Practical Handbook, Annex 25 (verbatim). For 8 days ≈ €384 — cash, card, or traveller's cheques all count (Borders Code Art. 6(4)). Carry your bank card + some cash; you're unlikely to be asked, but it's the written standard.
Accommodation + return ticket Annex I of the Borders Code: lodging document and return/onward ticket Verified You'll hold the booking confirmation(s) Hotspawn provides — keep them accessible (paper or phone).
Currency declaration Declare cash (or negotiable instruments) of €10,000 or more equivalent Verified State Department Malta advisory. Not applicable if you travel card-light.

2The address question — answered

Short answer

Never give any government your home address for this trip — none of them ask for it. For the airline/booking forms Hotspawn's team uses, the address is a mailing/contact address: the address of the property you own is perfectly acceptable there, and using it instead of your driver's-licence address is legal and normal. Just keep two things consistent: (a) your name, date of birth and sex exactly match your passport (that's what identity verification actually checks), and (b) the address you give is real and can receive mail if a flight changes.

Who actually wants an address, and what the law says

WhoDo they need an address?What the rule says (primary source)
TSA Secure Flight (US departure screening) No. There is no address field. 49 CFR §1560.101 (eCFR): airlines must request full name, sex, date of birth, and Redress Number only. No address element exists in the Passenger Screening Passenger Data.
US Customs (APIS arrival manifest, returning on your flight) No, for you. 19 CFR §122.49a(b)(3)(xii) (eCFR, re-read 24 Sep 2026): “Address while in the United States … except that this information is not required for U.S. citizens, lawful permanent residents, or persons in transit to a location outside the United States.” The manifest does carry citizenship (iv) and country of residence (v) — for you both are simply “United States”; no street address is transmitted.
Malta / Schengen border (entry) They ask where you're staying in Malta — not your home address. Borders Code Annex I: lodging document (your hotel booking) + return ticket. Your US home address is not a Schengen entry field.
Airline / travel agent booking forms (what Hotspawn will send you) Yes — but it's a correspondence address, not a government verification. No law requires it to match your driver's licence. Airlines use it for change notices and (rarely) baggage/identity correspondence. It's fine if it's a property you own, provided mail can reach you there or you forward it.
Your bank / card issuer Only the billing address on file with the bank matters for card payments. Unrelated to this trip's paperwork, but relevant if the prize provider ever asks for a card: billing address must match what the bank has.

Recommended response to Hotspawn's “Address” request

  • Send the address of the property you own (or your PO box / forwarding address) as your mailing address. It's real, it's yours, and it protects your home address's visibility in airline records.
  • Keep name and date of birth character-for-character identical to your passport (including middle names as they appear) — that's the only thing that gets cross-checked against an ID at the gate and border.
  • Where a form asks for “residential address” or “country of residence” in a government or compliance context, answer with your actual residence (California) — but no form in this trip's chain does. The giveaway's eligibility check (proof of residence, Terms §1.6/§8.3) is a prize-eligibility document request, not an immigration one — and that's exactly the document to hold back until the written waiver exists (see Hotspawn & Prize).
  • Phone number: give one that works while you're in Malta (you'll likely switch to a local SIM/eSIM — the US number must still be reachable for any SMS verification the airline uses at booking time).

3What to carry through the border

  • Passport (valid 3+ months past 20 Oct; issued within the last 10 years — Art. 6(1))
  • Hotel/flight booking confirmations (from Hotspawn, or screenshots if emailed)
  • TWC ticket confirmation (once issued)
  • Bank card + some cash (€48/day × 8 ≈ €384 standard, Art. 6(4))
  • Printed/screenshot of this page's §1 (useful to a curious officer; they'll ask standard questions: purpose = attending an esports tournament + tourism, duration = 7 nights, where staying = the prize hotel)
